Nutrition supports energy, tissue repair, immune function, hormone production, bone health, and disease prevention. A balanced pattern usually emphasizes vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, lean proteins, fish, and unsaturated fats. Highly processed foods, excess added sugar, high sodium intake, and frequent saturated or trans fats can increase cardiometabolic risk. Protein needs vary by age, activity, pregnancy, illness, and kidney function. Fiber supports bowel health, cholesterol control, glucose regulation, and fullness. Hydration needs depend on climate, activity, fever, pregnancy, kidney function, and medicines. Micronutrients such as iron, vitamin B12, folate, calcium, vitamin D, iodine, and zinc are important, and deficiency patterns differ across diets and life stages. Nutrition advice should respect culture, budget, access, preferences, and medical conditions. For diabetes, kidney disease, heart failure, celiac disease, pregnancy, or eating disorders, individualized professional guidance is important. Sustainable changes usually beat extreme short term diets. This document is educational.
