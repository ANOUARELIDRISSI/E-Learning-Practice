Prenatal care monitors the health of a pregnant person and developing fetus, identifies risks, and supports preparation for birth and parenting. Early care includes confirming pregnancy dating, reviewing medical history, medicines, allergies, prior pregnancies, genetic risks, vaccines, nutrition, substance use, and safety. Routine testing may include blood type, anemia screening, infection screening, urine testing, ultrasound, gestational diabetes screening, and blood pressure monitoring. Folic acid before and during early pregnancy reduces neural tube defect risk. Prenatal nutrition emphasizes adequate calories, protein, iron, calcium, iodine, folate, vitamin D, and safe food practices. Warning signs include heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, severe headache, vision changes, swelling with high blood pressure symptoms, fever, decreased fetal movement later in pregnancy, leaking fluid, and contractions before term. Avoiding tobacco, alcohol, and unsafe drugs is important. Some medicines are safe while others require adjustment, so medication review matters. This document is educational and not individualized prenatal advice.

For RAG practice, useful retrieval clues include condition name, symptoms, risk factors, complications, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment. A learner should compare lifestyle measures with medicine classes and notice when urgent evaluation is needed. The assistant should answer from the document, cite the filename, and avoid giving personal dosing or replacing a clinician.
