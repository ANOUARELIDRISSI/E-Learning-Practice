Heart failure is a clinical syndrome in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs or can do so only at high filling pressures. It does not mean the heart has stopped. Symptoms may include shortness of breath, swelling in the legs, fatigue, reduced exercise capacity, rapid weight gain from fluid, cough when lying down, and waking at night breathless. Causes include coronary artery disease, long standing hypertension, valve disease, cardiomyopathy, diabetes, infections, toxins, and rhythm problems. Diagnosis may involve examination, natriuretic peptide blood tests, electrocardiogram, chest imaging, and echocardiography. Treatment often includes sodium awareness, fluid management in selected patients, daily weights, exercise rehabilitation, and medicines that improve symptoms and survival. Common medicine groups include diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARNI therapy, beta blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, and SGLT2 inhibitors. Some patients need devices or procedures. Sudden worsening, chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, or pink frothy sputum requires urgent care. This document is educational.
