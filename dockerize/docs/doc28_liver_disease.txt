Liver disease includes many conditions that affect liver structure or function. The liver processes nutrients, produces bile, makes proteins, stores glycogen, metabolizes medicines, and helps clear toxins. Causes of liver disease include viral hepatitis, alcohol related injury, metabolic dysfunction associated steatotic liver disease, autoimmune hepatitis, genetic disorders, bile duct disease, medicines, toxins, and cancer. Early liver disease may cause no symptoms. Later signs can include fatigue, itching, abdominal swelling, leg swelling, yellow skin or eyes, easy bruising, confusion, vomiting blood, or black stools. Evaluation may include liver enzyme tests, bilirubin, albumin, clotting tests, viral studies, autoimmune markers, imaging, elastography, and sometimes biopsy. Treatment depends on cause and severity. Prevention includes vaccination for hepatitis A and B when indicated, limiting alcohol, weight management, safe injection practices, avoiding unnecessary toxins, and reviewing medication safety. Cirrhosis is advanced scarring that can lead to portal hypertension and liver failure. This document is educational only.
