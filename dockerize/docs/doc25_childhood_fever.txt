Fever in children is a rise in body temperature, usually reflecting immune response to infection. Many childhood fevers are caused by viral illnesses and improve with supportive care. Assessment should consider age, appearance, hydration, breathing, rash, immune status, vaccination status, and associated symptoms. Infants under three months with fever need prompt medical evaluation because serious infection can be harder to detect. Warning signs include difficulty breathing, bluish color, persistent vomiting, stiff neck, seizure, severe lethargy, inconsolable crying, dehydration, non blanching rash, or fever in a child with immune suppression. Fever reducing medicines such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen can improve comfort when used at correct weight based doses, but aspirin is generally avoided in children because of Reye syndrome risk. The number on the thermometer is less important than the child's overall condition. Fluids, rest, and monitoring are often helpful. Antibiotics are not useful for most viral infections.
