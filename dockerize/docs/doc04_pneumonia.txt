Pneumonia is an infection of the lung tissue, especially the air sacs where oxygen exchange occurs. It can be caused by bacteria, viruses, fungi, or aspiration of material into the lungs. Symptoms may include fever, chills, cough, sputum production, chest pain with breathing, shortness of breath, fatigue, confusion in older adults, and low oxygen levels. Diagnosis may involve physical examination, pulse oximetry, chest imaging, blood tests, and sputum studies when needed. Treatment depends on the cause, severity, age, immune status, and other medical conditions. Bacterial pneumonia is often treated with antibiotics, while viral pneumonia may require supportive care or antiviral therapy in selected cases. Warning signs include difficulty breathing, persistent chest pain, bluish lips, severe weakness, dehydration, and confusion. Prevention includes vaccination against influenza, pneumococcus, COVID-19, and other respiratory infections when recommended. Hand hygiene, smoking cessation, and managing chronic illness reduce risk. Pneumonia can be mild or life threatening, so medical evaluation matters. This document is for learning only.
