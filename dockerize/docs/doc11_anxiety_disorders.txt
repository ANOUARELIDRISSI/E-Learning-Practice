Anxiety disorders involve excessive fear, worry, or physical arousal that interferes with daily life. Normal anxiety can be useful when it alerts a person to danger or motivates preparation. A disorder is considered when anxiety becomes persistent, disproportionate, difficult to control, or disabling. Types include generalized anxiety disorder, panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, specific phobias, separation anxiety, and agoraphobia. Symptoms may include racing thoughts, restlessness, irritability, muscle tension, poor sleep, rapid heartbeat, sweating, trembling, chest tightness, dizziness, stomach upset, and avoidance. Diagnosis depends on history, symptom pattern, duration, impairment, and exclusion of medical or substance related causes. Treatment often includes cognitive behavioral therapy, exposure based strategies, mindfulness skills, sleep improvement, reduced caffeine, exercise, and medication when appropriate. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and serotonin norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors are commonly used. Panic symptoms can resemble heart or lung problems, so new or severe symptoms deserve medical evaluation. This course text is educational only.
