Obesity is a chronic, complex condition involving excess body fat that can affect health. It is influenced by genetics, appetite regulation, food environment, sleep, stress, medications, endocrine disorders, mobility limits, socioeconomic factors, and behavior. Body mass index is often used for screening, but it does not directly measure fat distribution, muscle mass, or individual health. Obesity can increase risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, sleep apnea, fatty liver disease, osteoarthritis, infertility, some cancers, and cardiovascular disease. Treatment should avoid blame and focus on health, function, and sustainable habits. Lifestyle care may include nutrition changes, physical activity, sleep improvement, behavioral strategies, and addressing emotional eating. Anti obesity medicines can help selected patients by changing appetite, fullness, or absorption. Bariatric surgery may be appropriate for some people with severe obesity or obesity related complications. Weight bias can harm care and discourage treatment. Even modest weight loss can improve blood pressure, glucose, triglycerides, and symptoms. This document is educational only.
