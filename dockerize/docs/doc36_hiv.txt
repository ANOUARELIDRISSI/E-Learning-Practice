Human immunodeficiency virus, or HIV, attacks CD4 immune cells and can weaken the body's ability to fight infections if untreated. HIV spreads through blood, semen, vaginal fluids, rectal fluids, and breast milk, most commonly through sex or shared injection equipment. It is not spread by casual contact, hugging, sharing food, or mosquitoes. Early infection may cause fever, rash, sore throat, swollen glands, or flu like symptoms, but many people have no symptoms. Diagnosis uses antigen antibody tests and confirmatory testing. Antiretroviral therapy suppresses viral replication, protects the immune system, and allows many people with HIV to live long healthy lives. When viral load is durably undetectable, sexual transmission does not occur, summarized as U equals U. Prevention includes condoms, sterile injection equipment, pre exposure prophylaxis, post exposure prophylaxis, testing, and treatment during pregnancy to prevent transmission to infants. Stigma is harmful and discourages testing and care. This document is educational only.
