Anemia occurs when the blood has too few healthy red blood cells or too little hemoglobin to carry oxygen effectively. Hemoglobin is the iron containing protein that gives red blood cells their oxygen carrying capacity. Symptoms can include fatigue, weakness, shortness of breath with activity, dizziness, pale skin, rapid heartbeat, headache, cold hands, and reduced exercise tolerance. Causes are broad. Iron deficiency from blood loss or low intake is common. Other causes include vitamin B12 deficiency, folate deficiency, chronic kidney disease, inflammation, inherited disorders, bone marrow disease, and hemolysis. Evaluation often includes a complete blood count, red cell indices, ferritin, iron studies, reticulocyte count, vitamin levels, kidney tests, and sometimes stool testing or endoscopy if blood loss is suspected. Treatment depends on the cause: iron replacement, vitamin replacement, treating bleeding, managing chronic disease, or transfusion in severe cases. Taking iron with vitamin C may improve absorption, while calcium can reduce it. This document teaches general concepts and is not a diagnosis.
