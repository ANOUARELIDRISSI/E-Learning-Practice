Meningitis is inflammation of the membranes surrounding the brain and spinal cord. It can be caused by viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites, autoimmune disease, or medications. Viral meningitis is often less severe, while bacterial meningitis can progress rapidly and become life threatening. Symptoms may include fever, severe headache, stiff neck, sensitivity to light, vomiting, confusion, sleepiness, seizure, or rash. Infants may show poor feeding, irritability, lethargy, bulging fontanelle, or abnormal temperature. Diagnosis may require blood tests, imaging when needed, and lumbar puncture to examine cerebrospinal fluid. Bacterial meningitis is treated urgently with intravenous antibiotics and sometimes corticosteroids. Delays can increase the risk of hearing loss, brain injury, shock, and death. Prevention includes vaccination against organisms such as meningococcus, pneumococcus, and Haemophilus influenzae type b when recommended. Close contacts of certain bacterial meningitis cases may need preventive antibiotics. Suspected meningitis requires emergency medical evaluation. This document is educational only.
