Pain management aims to reduce suffering, improve function, and treat underlying causes when possible. Pain may be acute, such as after injury or surgery, or chronic, lasting beyond normal healing. It may arise from tissue injury, inflammation, nerve dysfunction, central sensitization, or mixed mechanisms. Assessment includes location, intensity, quality, triggers, timing, functional impact, mood, sleep, medicines, and red flags. Treatment should be individualized. Non medicine strategies include physical therapy, movement, heat, cold, relaxation, cognitive behavioral therapy, sleep improvement, weight management, and ergonomic changes. Medicines may include acetaminophen, nonsteroidal anti inflammatory drugs, topical agents, antidepressants for neuropathic pain, anticonvulsants for nerve pain, muscle relaxants in selected cases, and opioids for limited situations. Opioids can be useful for severe acute pain or cancer pain but carry risks of sedation, constipation, dependence, overdose, and misuse. Chronic pain care often focuses on function rather than complete elimination of pain. This document is educational only.
