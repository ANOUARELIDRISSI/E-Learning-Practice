Hypertension means persistently elevated blood pressure in the arteries. It is often called a silent condition because many people feel normal while the heart, brain, kidneys, and blood vessels experience extra strain. Blood pressure is recorded with two numbers. The systolic number reflects pressure when the heart contracts, and the diastolic number reflects pressure when the heart relaxes. Long term hypertension increases the risk of stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney disease, vision problems, and vascular damage. Common risk factors include age, family history, high salt intake, obesity, inactivity, excess alcohol, smoking, chronic stress, sleep apnea, diabetes, and kidney disease. Lifestyle treatment may include reducing sodium, eating more fruits and vegetables, regular aerobic activity, weight management, limiting alcohol, stopping tobacco, and improving sleep. Medicines can include thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, calcium channel blockers, and other agents. Accurate measurement matters: the person should rest, use a proper cuff, support the arm, and repeat readings. This course document is educational and not personal medical advice.
