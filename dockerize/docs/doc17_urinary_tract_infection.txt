Urinary tract infection, or UTI, occurs when microbes infect part of the urinary system. Lower UTI, called cystitis, affects the bladder. Symptoms may include burning with urination, urinary frequency, urgency, lower abdominal discomfort, cloudy urine, or blood in urine. Kidney infection, called pyelonephritis, may cause fever, chills, flank pain, nausea, vomiting, and feeling very ill. Most uncomplicated UTIs are caused by bacteria from the bowel, especially Escherichia coli. Risk factors include sexual activity, pregnancy, urinary obstruction, catheter use, diabetes, kidney stones, and postmenopausal changes. Diagnosis may be based on symptoms and urine testing. Urine culture is useful in complicated cases, recurrent infection, pregnancy, or treatment failure. Treatment depends on patient factors and local resistance patterns. Drinking fluids may help comfort, but antibiotics are often required for bacterial infection. Warning signs include fever, back pain, pregnancy, immune suppression, male UTI symptoms, or persistent symptoms after treatment. This document provides general education only.

For RAG practice, useful retrieval clues include condition name, symptoms, risk factors, complications, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment. A learner should compare lifestyle measures with medicine classes and notice when urgent evaluation is needed. The assistant should answer from the document, cite the filename, and avoid giving personal dosing or replacing a clinician.
