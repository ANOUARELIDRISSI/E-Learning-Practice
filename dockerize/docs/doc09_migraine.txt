Migraine is a neurologic disorder that causes recurring attacks of head pain and sensory sensitivity. Migraine pain is often throbbing, moderate to severe, and may affect one side of the head, although patterns vary. Associated symptoms can include nausea, vomiting, sensitivity to light, sensitivity to sound, dizziness, neck discomfort, and difficulty thinking clearly. Some people experience aura, such as flashing lights, blind spots, tingling, or speech disturbance before or during an attack. Triggers may include sleep disruption, stress, skipped meals, dehydration, alcohol, hormonal changes, weather shifts, certain foods, and bright light. Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on history and examination. Treatment includes acute medicines taken during attacks and preventive strategies for frequent or disabling migraine. Acute options may include NSAIDs, acetaminophen, triptans, anti nausea medicines, gepants, or ditans depending on the patient. Prevention may include lifestyle routines, beta blockers, antiepileptic medicines, antidepressants, CGRP targeted therapies, or botulinum toxin. Sudden worst headache needs urgent evaluation.
