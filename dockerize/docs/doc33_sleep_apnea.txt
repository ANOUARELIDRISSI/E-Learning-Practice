Sleep apnea is a sleep related breathing disorder in which breathing repeatedly stops or becomes shallow during sleep. Obstructive sleep apnea occurs when the upper airway collapses or narrows. Symptoms include loud snoring, witnessed pauses in breathing, gasping, morning headaches, dry mouth, daytime sleepiness, poor concentration, irritability, and unrefreshing sleep. Risk factors include obesity, older age, male sex, large neck circumference, nasal obstruction, alcohol or sedative use, and certain jaw or airway anatomy. Untreated sleep apnea can contribute to hypertension, heart rhythm problems, stroke risk, insulin resistance, motor vehicle accidents, and reduced quality of life. Diagnosis is made with sleep testing at home or in a sleep lab. Treatment may include weight management, avoiding alcohol near bedtime, side sleeping, nasal therapy, oral appliances, positive airway pressure therapy, or surgery for selected patients. Continuous positive airway pressure keeps the airway open and is highly effective when used regularly. This document is educational only.
