Allergies occur when the immune system reacts to a normally harmless substance such as pollen, dust mites, animal dander, mold, foods, medicines, or insect venom. Allergic rhinitis can cause sneezing, itchy nose, runny nose, congestion, and itchy watery eyes. Skin allergies may cause hives, eczema flares, swelling, or itching. Food allergy may cause hives, vomiting, wheezing, throat tightness, or anaphylaxis. Diagnosis begins with a careful history because timing, exposure, and symptom pattern matter. Skin prick testing or blood tests for specific IgE can support diagnosis but must be interpreted in context. Treatment includes allergen avoidance, nasal saline, antihistamines, nasal corticosteroids, eye drops, inhalers for allergic asthma, and immunotherapy in selected cases. Anaphylaxis is a medical emergency and is treated with epinephrine. People with serious allergies may need an epinephrine auto injector and an action plan. Not all intolerances are immune allergies.
