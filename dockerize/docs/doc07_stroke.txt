Stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is interrupted or when bleeding damages brain tissue. Ischemic stroke is caused by a blocked artery, while hemorrhagic stroke is caused by bleeding. Symptoms often start suddenly. They can include facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty, vision loss, severe dizziness, trouble walking, confusion, numbness, or a sudden intense headache. The FAST reminder highlights Face, Arm, Speech, and Time because emergency treatment is time sensitive. Diagnosis commonly uses brain imaging such as CT or MRI, along with blood tests, heart rhythm evaluation, and vascular imaging. Treatment depends on stroke type and timing. Some ischemic strokes may be treated with clot dissolving medicine or mechanical clot removal. Hemorrhagic strokes may require blood pressure control, reversal of blood thinners, or neurosurgical care. Prevention focuses on controlling blood pressure, diabetes, cholesterol, smoking, atrial fibrillation, diet, activity, and weight. Rehabilitation helps restore function and independence. This document is for education, not emergency guidance.

For RAG practice, useful retrieval clues include condition name, symptoms, risk factors, complications, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment. A learner should compare lifestyle measures with medicine classes and notice when urgent evaluation is needed.
