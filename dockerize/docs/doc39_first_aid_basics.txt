First aid provides immediate support for illness or injury until professional care is available. The first step is scene safety: do not become another injured person. Check responsiveness, breathing, and severe bleeding. Call emergency services for unconsciousness, severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, stroke symptoms, major trauma, severe burns, poisoning, seizure lasting more than five minutes, or uncontrolled bleeding. For heavy bleeding, apply firm direct pressure with clean material. For burns, cool the area with running water and avoid ice directly on skin. For suspected fracture, immobilize the area and avoid unnecessary movement. For choking in a conscious adult who cannot cough or speak, abdominal thrusts may be used by trained helpers. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation and automated external defibrillator use improve survival in cardiac arrest. First aid kits should contain gloves, dressings, bandages, antiseptic, scissors, tweezers, and emergency numbers. Training improves confidence and effectiveness. This document is educational and does not replace certified first aid instruction.

For RAG practice, useful retrieval clues include condition name, symptoms, risk factors, complications, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment.
