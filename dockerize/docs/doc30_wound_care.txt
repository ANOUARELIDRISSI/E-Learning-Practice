Wound care supports healing and prevents infection. Basic assessment includes wound cause, depth, bleeding, contamination, location, pain, circulation, sensation, tetanus status, and patient factors such as diabetes, smoking, nutrition, or immune suppression. Small clean cuts may be washed with running water, gently cleaned, covered with a sterile dressing, and monitored. Deep wounds, animal bites, human bites, dirty punctures, heavy bleeding, wounds with foreign bodies, and wounds over joints may need medical care. Signs of infection include increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, worsening pain, fever, and red streaking. Moist wound healing with appropriate dressings often works better than letting wounds dry out completely. Pressure injuries require pressure relief, nutrition support, moisture control, and treatment of infection when present. Chronic wounds may need vascular assessment, glucose control, debridement, compression for venous disease, or specialized dressings. Tetanus vaccination should be updated when indicated.
