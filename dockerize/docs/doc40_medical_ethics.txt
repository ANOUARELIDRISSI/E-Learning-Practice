Medical ethics guides decisions in health care by balancing values, duties, evidence, and patient rights. Four common principles are autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence, and justice. Autonomy means respecting a capable patient's right to make informed decisions. Beneficence means acting for the patient's welfare. Nonmaleficence means avoiding unnecessary harm. Justice concerns fairness, access, and responsible use of resources. Informed consent requires understandable information about benefits, risks, alternatives, and the option to refuse. Confidentiality protects patient privacy, with exceptions when required by law or serious safety concerns. Capacity is decision specific and can change over time. Ethical conflicts can arise around end of life care, scarce resources, research, reproductive health, refusal of treatment, and cultural differences. Shared decision making combines clinical expertise with patient goals and values. Documentation and ethics consultation can help complex cases. Good ethics is not only theory; it appears in respectful listening and honest communication. This document is educational.
