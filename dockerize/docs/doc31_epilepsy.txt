Epilepsy is a disorder of recurrent unprovoked seizures caused by abnormal electrical activity in the brain. Seizures can look different depending on where they start and how they spread. Some cause brief staring, confusion, unusual sensations, repetitive movements, muscle jerking, falls, or loss of consciousness. A single seizure does not always mean epilepsy; fever, low glucose, alcohol withdrawal, head injury, infection, or electrolyte problems can provoke seizures. Evaluation may include history from witnesses, neurological examination, blood tests, electroencephalogram, and brain imaging. Treatment often uses antiseizure medicines chosen by seizure type, age, pregnancy considerations, other conditions, and side effects. Some people benefit from surgery, nerve stimulation, or dietary therapy. Safety counseling includes driving laws, swimming supervision, heights, machinery, and seizure first aid. During a convulsive seizure, protect the person from injury, place them on their side when possible, time the seizure, and do not put anything in the mouth. This document is educational.
