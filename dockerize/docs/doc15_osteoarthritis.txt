Osteoarthritis is a joint disease involving cartilage breakdown, bone remodeling, low grade inflammation, and changes in surrounding tissues. It commonly affects knees, hips, hands, spine, and feet. Symptoms include joint pain with activity, stiffness after rest, reduced range of motion, swelling, creaking sensations, and functional limitation. Morning stiffness is usually brief compared with inflammatory arthritis. Risk factors include aging, previous joint injury, obesity, repetitive loading, muscle weakness, genetics, and joint alignment problems. Diagnosis is usually based on symptoms and examination, with X rays used when needed. Treatment focuses on improving function and reducing pain. Exercise is one of the most useful therapies, especially strengthening and low impact aerobic activity. Weight reduction can reduce knee and hip load. Physical therapy, braces, heat, cold, topical NSAIDs, oral pain medicines, injections, and assistive devices may help. Joint replacement is considered when severe disease limits life despite conservative treatment. Pain severity does not always match imaging findings. This document is educational.
