Depression is a common mental health condition that affects mood, thinking, body energy, sleep, appetite, motivation, and daily functioning. It is more than ordinary sadness. Symptoms may include persistent low mood, loss of interest, guilt, hopelessness, fatigue, concentration difficulty, sleep changes, appetite changes, slowed movement, agitation, and thoughts of death or self harm. Causes are complex and may involve genetics, life stress, trauma, medical illness, medications, substance use, social isolation, and brain chemistry. Diagnosis is based on a careful clinical interview and screening for medical contributors such as thyroid disease, anemia, or substance use. Treatment can include psychotherapy, behavioral activation, exercise, social support, sleep routines, treatment of medical problems, and antidepressant medication. Cognitive behavioral therapy and interpersonal therapy are examples of evidence based approaches. Severe depression, psychosis, inability to care for oneself, or suicidal thoughts require urgent professional support. Recovery often takes time and adjustment of treatment. This document is educational and not a substitute for mental health care.
