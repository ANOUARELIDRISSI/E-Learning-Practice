Tuberculosis, or TB, is an infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It most often affects the lungs but can involve lymph nodes, bones, kidneys, brain, or other organs. TB spreads through airborne particles when someone with infectious lung or throat TB coughs, speaks, or sings. Latent TB infection means the bacteria are present but inactive, causing no symptoms and not spreading to others. Active TB disease can cause cough lasting weeks, fever, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue, chest pain, or coughing blood. Diagnosis may include skin testing, blood interferon gamma release assays, chest imaging, sputum microscopy, culture, and molecular tests. Treatment requires multiple antibiotics for months because TB grows slowly and resistance can develop. Completing therapy is essential. Drug resistant TB needs specialized regimens. Public health teams help with contact tracing, treatment support, and prevention. People with HIV, immune suppression, malnutrition, or close exposure have higher risk of disease.
