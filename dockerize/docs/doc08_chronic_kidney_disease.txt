Chronic kidney disease, or CKD, means gradual loss of kidney function over months to years. Kidneys filter waste, balance electrolytes, regulate fluid, support blood pressure control, and help maintain red blood cell production and bone health. Early CKD may cause no symptoms. Later symptoms can include swelling, fatigue, nausea, itching, muscle cramps, changes in urination, high blood pressure, and trouble concentrating. Common causes include diabetes, hypertension, glomerular diseases, polycystic kidney disease, recurrent kidney infections, urinary obstruction, and some medicines or toxins. Diagnosis uses estimated glomerular filtration rate from blood creatinine and urine albumin testing. Management aims to slow progression and reduce cardiovascular risk. Important steps include blood pressure control, glucose control, avoiding smoking, reviewing medicines, limiting harmful anti inflammatory drugs when possible, and using kidney protective medicines in appropriate patients. Advanced CKD may require dialysis or kidney transplantation. Nutrition advice may include sodium, protein, potassium, or phosphorus adjustments depending on stage. This document is educational only.
