Skin infections can involve the surface, hair follicles, deeper skin layers, or soft tissue. Bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites can all infect skin. Cellulitis is a bacterial infection of deeper skin that causes redness, warmth, swelling, tenderness, and sometimes fever. Impetigo causes honey colored crusts, often in children. Abscesses are pockets of pus that may need drainage. Fungal infections such as ringworm often cause scaly circular patches, while athlete's foot affects the feet. Risk factors include cuts, eczema, diabetes, poor circulation, immune suppression, crowded conditions, and shared personal items. Diagnosis is often clinical, but cultures, scrapings, or imaging may be needed. Treatment depends on the organism and depth of infection. Antibiotics, antifungals, antivirals, drainage, wound care, and hygiene measures may be used. Warning signs include rapidly spreading redness, severe pain, fever, red streaks, immune suppression, infection near the eye, or blackened skin. This document is educational and not a substitute for care.
