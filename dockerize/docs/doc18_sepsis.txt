Sepsis is a life threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated response to infection. It can begin from infections in the lungs, urinary tract, abdomen, skin, bloodstream, or other sites. Early recognition is critical because outcomes improve with rapid treatment. Symptoms can include fever or low temperature, fast heart rate, rapid breathing, confusion, severe weakness, low blood pressure, reduced urination, mottled skin, and worsening pain or discomfort. Older adults and immunocompromised people may have subtle symptoms. Diagnosis involves clinical assessment, vital signs, blood tests, cultures, imaging, and identifying the infection source. Treatment usually includes prompt antibiotics, intravenous fluids, oxygen, source control such as drainage or surgery when needed, and support for failing organs. Septic shock is sepsis with persistent circulatory failure and high mortality risk. Prevention includes vaccination, infection control, early treatment of infections, careful catheter use, and chronic disease management. Sepsis is an emergency, not a wait and watch condition.
