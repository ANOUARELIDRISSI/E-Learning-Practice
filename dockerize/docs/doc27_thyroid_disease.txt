The thyroid gland produces hormones that regulate metabolism, temperature, heart rate, digestion, mood, and energy use. Hypothyroidism means too little thyroid hormone. Symptoms may include fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, weight gain, slow heart rate, depression, heavy periods, and hair thinning. Hashimoto thyroiditis is a common cause. Hyperthyroidism means too much thyroid hormone. Symptoms may include weight loss, heat intolerance, tremor, anxiety, palpitations, frequent stools, sweating, insomnia, and muscle weakness. Graves disease, thyroid nodules, and thyroiditis are possible causes. Testing usually begins with thyroid stimulating hormone, or TSH, and free thyroxine, or free T4. Treatment for hypothyroidism commonly uses levothyroxine, with dosing guided by lab monitoring. Hyperthyroidism treatment may include antithyroid medicines, radioactive iodine, beta blockers for symptoms, or surgery depending on cause and patient factors. Thyroid disease in pregnancy requires careful monitoring. This course document is educational and does not replace clinical evaluation.
