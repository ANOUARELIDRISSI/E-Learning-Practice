Dementia describes acquired decline in memory, thinking, behavior, or daily function severe enough to interfere with independence. Alzheimer disease is the most common cause, but vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, frontotemporal dementia, Parkinson disease dementia, and reversible contributors must be considered. Symptoms may include memory loss, language difficulty, poor judgment, getting lost, personality change, hallucinations, sleep disruption, apathy, or problems managing finances and medicines. Evaluation includes history from the patient and someone who knows them well, cognitive testing, medication review, mood screening, neurological examination, blood tests, and brain imaging when appropriate. Some conditions mimic or worsen dementia, including depression, sleep apnea, thyroid disease, vitamin B12 deficiency, infection, medication effects, and hearing loss. Management includes safety planning, exercise, cognitive and social activity, caregiver support, sleep routines, treatment of vascular risk factors, and medicines for selected diagnoses. Planning for driving, finances, advance directives, and home safety is important. This document is educational only.

For RAG practice, useful retrieval clues include condition name, symptoms, risk factors, complications, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment. A learner should compare lifestyle measures with medicine classes and notice when urgent evaluation is needed. The assistant should answer from the document, cite the filename, and avoid giving personal dosing or replacing a clinician.
