Rheumatoid arthritis is an autoimmune inflammatory disease that primarily attacks the lining of joints. It often causes symmetric pain, swelling, warmth, and stiffness in small joints of the hands and feet, although many joints and organs can be affected. Morning stiffness lasting more than an hour is common. Fatigue, low grade fever, nodules, eye inflammation, lung involvement, and anemia may occur. Without treatment, ongoing inflammation can damage cartilage and bone, leading to deformity and disability. Diagnosis uses history, examination, inflammatory markers, rheumatoid factor, anti CCP antibodies, and imaging when appropriate. Early treatment matters because disease modifying antirheumatic drugs can slow or prevent joint damage. Methotrexate is commonly used, and biologic or targeted synthetic medicines may be added for persistent disease. Nonsteroidal anti inflammatory drugs and corticosteroids can reduce symptoms but do not replace disease modifying therapy. Monitoring is needed for medicine safety and disease control.
