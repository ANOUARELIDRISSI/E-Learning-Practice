Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria change in ways that make antibiotic medicines less effective. It can develop through genetic mutation or acquisition of resistance genes from other bacteria. Antibiotic use creates selection pressure: susceptible bacteria die, while resistant bacteria survive and multiply. Resistance can spread in communities, hospitals, farms, and the environment. Consequences include longer illness, more complications, higher costs, increased mortality, and fewer effective treatment options. Misuse includes taking antibiotics for viral infections, stopping treatment too early when a clinician prescribed a specific course, using leftover medicines, and unnecessary broad spectrum prescribing. Stewardship means using the right antibiotic only when needed, at the right dose, route, and duration. Infection prevention also matters: hand hygiene, vaccination, safe food handling, sanitation, and isolation practices reduce spread. Diagnostic testing can help distinguish bacterial from viral illness and guide therapy. Patients should not pressure clinicians for antibiotics when they are unlikely to help.
