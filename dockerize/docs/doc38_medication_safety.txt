Medication safety means using medicines in ways that maximize benefit and reduce harm. Important habits include knowing each medicine name, dose, schedule, purpose, and possible side effects. Patients should tell clinicians about prescriptions, over the counter products, vitamins, supplements, allergies, pregnancy, kidney disease, liver disease, and alcohol or substance use. Drug interactions can occur when one medicine changes the effect of another, or when foods and supplements interfere. Some medicines require lab monitoring, blood pressure checks, glucose checks, or dose adjustment for kidney function. Antibiotics, blood thinners, insulin, opioids, sedatives, and chemotherapy agents require special attention. Do not crush extended release tablets unless a pharmacist says it is safe. Store medicines away from children, heat, moisture, and pets. Dispose of unused medicines through take back programs when possible. Medication reconciliation at every visit reduces duplication and errors. New confusion, rash, breathing trouble, severe dizziness, or swelling after medicine can require urgent care.
